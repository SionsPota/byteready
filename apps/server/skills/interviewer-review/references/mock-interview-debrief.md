# Mock Interview Debrief Methodology

How to conduct and debrief a simulated interview session.

## Pre-Mock Setup

Before starting the mock, clarify:
- Target role and level
- Target company type (big tech, startup, etc.)
- Which round to simulate (technical, system design, behavioral, HR)
- Specific concerns the candidate wants to work on
- Time available (recommend 45-60 min for full experience)

## The Mock Interview Flow

### Opening (2 min)
Stay in character as the interviewer persona. Set expectations:
> "This is a mock [round type] interview for a [level] [role] position.
> I'll treat this as a real interview for the first [X] minutes, then we'll
> debrief. Ready?"

### The Interview (30-45 min)
Conduct the interview following the appropriate persona from the
interviewer-persona skill. Take mental or written notes on:
- Where they struggled
- Where they excelled
- Follow-up questions they could have handled better
- Communication patterns (rambling, too terse, etc.)

### Immediate Debrief (15-20 min)

Switch out of character immediately:
> "OK, interview's over — let's talk about how that went."

Structure the debrief in this order:

#### 1. Emotional Temperature Check (1 min)
> "Before I give feedback — how do you think that went? What was your
> self-assessment?"

This reveals self-awareness and calibrates your feedback against their
own perception.

#### 2. The Verdict (1 min)
Give a clear simulated hiring decision:
> "If this were a real interview, my recommendation would be:
> [Strong Hire / Hire / Lean Hire / Lean No-Hire / No-Hire]."

Be specific about why:
> "You hit a strong hire on the coding question — clean solution,
> good edge cases, clear communication. But the system design round
> would be a lean hire — you jumped to implementation before fully
> understanding the requirements."

#### 3. Strengths (3-5 min)
List 2-3 specific strengths with evidence:
- "Your coding style is clean — I especially liked how you named the
  variables and added the helper function."
- "When I pushed back on your caching strategy, you defended it with
  data — that shows conviction."
- "The hook in your self-intro made me want to ask follow-ups."

#### 4. Growth Areas (5-8 min)
List 2-3 priority improvements. For each:
- What the issue was
- Why it matters to interviewers
- How to fix it (with specific example)

Example:
> **Issue**: You took 90 seconds to answer "Why our company?" and the
> first 60 seconds were generic.
>
> **Why it matters**: By the time you got to the specific reason, I'd
> already mentally categorized it as a stock answer.
>
> **Fix**: Lead with the specific: "I'm specifically interested in your
> [team] because [specific technical challenge or product]. The broader
> reason is [mission alignment]." Cut from 90 to 30 seconds.

#### 5. The Replay (5 min)
Pick one critical moment and "replay" it:
> "Let's rewind to the question about [X]. Here's what you said:
> [paraphrase]. Here's a stronger approach: [demonstrate]. Want to
> try answering it again?"

This is the highest-value part of the debrief — real-time improvement.

#### 6. Preparation Plan (3 min)
End with 3 specific action items:
1. **Study**: "Review [topic]. I asked about X and you were vague on Y."
2. **Practice**: "Do [N] mock problems focusing on [specific skill]."
3. **Behavioral**: "Prepare 2-3 STAR stories for [common question types]."

## Debrief Tone

- Direct but kind: "That answer would lose points" not "That was terrible"
- Evidence-based: cite specific moments from the mock
- Calibrated to their level: junior engineers get more benefit-of-doubt
- Focused on the highest-impact changes (don't overwhelm with 10 fixes)
- Time-conscious: stay within the allotted debrief window

## Common Mock Interview Patterns

### The Overconfident Candidate
- Signals: argues with feedback, defends clearly wrong answers
- Approach: soften the verdict but be firm on facts; focus on one
  high-impact change

### The Underconfident Candidate
- Signals: self-deprecating, apologizes frequently, doesn't own
  achievements
- Approach: emphasize strengths more than usual; give specific
  language to use (literally provide the words)

### The Over-Rehearsed Candidate
- Signals: sounds scripted, doesn't adapt to follow-ups, misses the
  intent behind questions
- Approach: encourage pausing and thinking; practice "structured
  improvisation"

### The Brilliant but Unclear Candidate
- Signals: technically strong but can't explain their thinking
- Approach: focus entirely on communication structure; the "say what
  you're doing and why" drill
