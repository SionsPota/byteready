# Self-Introduction Review Methodology

How to evaluate and improve a candidate's self-introduction from an
interviewer's perspective.

## Why the Self-Introduction Matters

The self-introduction is the most rehearsed 1-3 minutes of the interview.
Yet candidates routinely waste it. From the interviewer's perspective:
- It's the candidate's chance to **frame the narrative**
- It reveals **what they think is important** about their background
- It sets the **energy and confidence level** for the entire interview
- It provides **hooks** for the interviewer's next questions

A bad self-introduction puts the interviewer in a "dig out of this hole"
mindset. A great one makes them want to learn more.

## The Anatomy of a Strong Self-Introduction

### Structure (90-120 seconds for junior, 2-3 minutes for senior)

```
[10 sec] Hook — why you're here / what drives you
[20 sec] Current/most recent role — what you do + 1 highlight
[30 sec] Key previous roles — 1-2 highlights with outcomes
[15 sec] Education (if relevant) or transition context
[15 sec] Why this role/company — specific connection
[10 sec] Transition — "That's the overview. Happy to dive deeper."
```

### The Hook Patterns (Opening 10 Seconds)

**The problem-driven hook:**
> "I've spent the last 4 years obsessed with one problem: how do you
> scale real-time data pipelines without blowing up the infrastructure
> budget? That's what brought me here."

**The journey hook:**
> "I started as a backend engineer, but after being on-call for a system
> that went down 3 times in one month, I got obsessed with reliability
> engineering. That's been my focus ever since."

**The mission hook:**
> "I'm a product manager who believes the best features are the ones
> users don't notice because everything just works. I've shipped 3
> products from 0 to 1 with that philosophy."

**Bad hooks** (what interviewers hear too often):
- "So, I graduated from [school] in [year] with a degree in..." (boring)
- "My name is [name], I have X years of experience in..." (wasted 10 seconds)
- "I saw this job posting and thought I'd be a good fit because..." (too eager)

## Evaluation Criteria

### 1. Relevance Filtering
Does the candidate know what's relevant to *this* role?
- Mentioning a PhD thesis on graph theory for a frontend role: bad filter
- Mentioning a side project in React for a frontend role: good filter
- Spending 60 seconds on a job 8 years ago vs. 20 seconds on the current one:
  bad prioritization

### 2. "I" vs. "We" Ratio
Track the pronouns:
- **"I designed the architecture"** — clear ownership
- **"We built the system"** — unclear contribution
- Good ratio: roughly 70% "I" statements for individual contributions,
  30% "we" for team context

### 3. Specificity Density
Count the specific details per minute:
- Numbers ("reduced from 200ms to 15ms")
- Technologies ("implemented with Kafka Streams")
- Outcomes ("cut incident response time by 60%")
- Scope ("for 2M daily active users")

A strong self-introduction has 3-5 specific details per minute.
A weak one has generic claims ("improved performance significantly").

### 4. Energy and Confidence
Noted through word choice and pacing:
- "I believe I can contribute" (hesitant) vs. "I've solved similar
  problems and I'm excited to do it here" (confident)
- Over-apologizing: "Sorry, this might be too detailed..." (undermines)
- Rushing through: signals nervousness or over-rehearsal

### 5. The Transition
How they hand control back to the interviewer:
- **Good**: "That's the overview. I'm happy to dive into any part —
  technical details, project specifics, or why I'm making this move."
  (gives the interviewer options)
- **Weak**: "So... yeah, that's me." (awkward ending)
- **Presumptuous**: "I think we'd be a great fit." (too forward)

## The Review Process

### Step 1: Transcribe the Self-Introduction
If the user provides text, use it directly. If they provide a recording,
summarize the key points.

### Step 2: Mark the Structure
Label each section:
- [HOOK]
- [CURRENT ROLE]
- [PREVIOUS ROLES]
- [EDUCATION/CONTEXT]
- [WHY HERE]
- [TRANSITION]

Identify what's missing or out of proportion.

### Step 3: Highlight Specifics
Underline or bold every specific detail. If there are fewer than 3 in
the entire introduction, flag this as the top issue.

### Step 4: Rewrite
Provide a rewritten version that:
- Keeps their authentic voice (don't make it sound like someone else)
- Strengthens the hook
- Adds specific details where they're missing
- Fixes the "I"/"we" ratio
- Improves the transition
- Fits the time target (90-120 seconds for junior, 2-3 min for senior)

### Step 5: Practice Notes
Give 2-3 specific practice instructions:
- "Record yourself and cut anything that isn't a specific detail or
  a transition"
- "Practice the first 15 seconds until it feels natural, not rehearsed"
- "Time yourself — if you're over 3 minutes, cut your oldest role
  to 10 seconds"
